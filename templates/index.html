    When to split, and when not to

    Split

    Two or more policy areas that different documents cover. One retrieval over “how is my notice period paid, and does my insurance continue?” returns four chunks about notice and none about insurance, so half the question goes unanswered with no sign that it did.

    Do not split

    A single question with several clauses, or one asking for a number and its deadline. Splitting those buys an extra retrieval and an extra generation to answer the same thing twice — and two independently composed halves is how an answer ends up contradicting itself.

    The decision is a structured call with a safe default, and the safe default is do not split. A wrongly split question costs latency; a wrongly merged one costs the employee half their answer, but it reads as complete, which is the worse failure.

    Retrieval

    Documents are chunked at 1,000 characters with 150 of overlap, markdown split on headings first so a chunk begins at its section title. Each chunk carries source, title, doc_id, doc_type, department, origin and ingested_on — the metadata retrieval filters on. Chunk ids are keyed on position rather than content, so an edited chunk updates in place instead of leaving its previous version behind as a second vector.

    Three retrieval modes, resolved at startup from what the deployment has keys for: dense (Pinecone, cosine), lexical (BM25 over the same documents on disk, no keys and no network), and hybrid, which fuses the two with reciprocal rank fusion and then re-ranks. RRF fuses by rank rather than by score on purpose: a cosine similarity and a BM25 score are not on the same scale, and no amount of normalisation makes them comparable across queries.

    The gate is an out-of-domain filter, not a relevance grader

    Measured over a 12-question evaluation: out-of-domain questions peaked at 0.099, while the lowest-scoring correct-source chunk was 0.171. 0.15 sits in that gap. It cannot separate right-document from wrong-document — a wrong-source chunk has scored as high as 0.521 — so judging whether evidence actually answers the question stays the model’s job. Re-measured when the corpus grew from 6 documents to 11: out-of-domain still peaked at 0.078, so 0.15 still holds.

    The gate and the LLM grader are complementary rather than redundant. “Quokka grooming sabbatical” clears the gate — it looks lexically like leave policy — and the grader catches it. “What is the capital of France?” never reaches the grader at all. Removing either one opens a hole.

    RAG, fine-tuning, or a long context?

    All three are ways to get company facts into an answer, and this product needs the one where the facts can change on a Tuesday afternoon and be cited afterwards.

    ApproachWhy not here
    Fine-tuning Teaches style and format, not facts you can cite. A policy edit means a retraining run, the old answer stays in the weights, and nothing can show the employee the paragraph it came from.
    Whole corpus in context Works at this size (11 documents is a few tens of thousands of tokens) and stops working at the size an HR corpus actually reaches. It also pays for the whole handbook on every greeting, and attention degrades well before the context limit does.
    Retrieval A policy edit is an ingest. Cost is bounded by k rather than by corpus size. Every answer carries the documents it rests on, which is the part an HR answer cannot do without.

    The eleven stages around the graph

    The graph answers the question. The pipeline decides whether it should be answered, whether it already has been, which model should do it, and what has to be true of the answer before anyone sees it.

    1. Input guard — refuse, or redact and carry on.
    2. Semantic cache — an answer already given to this question.
    3. Model routing — complexity to tier to model id.
    4. Orchestration — graph, ReAct, or supervisor.
    5. Run — streamed stage by stage.
    6. Generation fallback — evidence but no model: extract rather than apologise.
    7. Output guard — redact what the model echoed; flag the rest.
    8. Accounting — tokens, projected cost, budget report.
    9. Cache store — only an answer worth repeating.

    The order is the design. A guard after the cache could poison it; a router before the cache pays for a classification it did not need; an output guard before generation guards nothing.

    Two things are the deployer’s decision

    The audit log is SQLite on a volume, so a platform with an ephemeral filesystem loses it on every redeploy — for an HR audit trail that is the failure the log exists to prevent. And /api/chat is unauthenticated by design, so a public URL needs a rate limit in front of it. Both are in the README’s deployment section; neither is something to quietly fix in a Dockerfile.

    Knowledge base

    Whoever can upload here can change what the company’s policy appears to say, which is why this is gated. A single-file upload never prunes: pruning reconciles a namespace against a directory, which for one file would delete the rest of the corpus.

    📄 Drop a policy document, or click to choose Markdown, plain text, PDF or DOCX

    Becomes chunk metadata and a retrieval filter value. Lower case, digits, dot, dash or underscore.
    An ingest drops the answer cache

    A new document can change the answer to a question that mentions none of its words, so a time-based expiry is not enough — fifteen minutes of serving superseded policy is exactly what this must not do.

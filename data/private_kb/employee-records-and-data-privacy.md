# Employee Records and Data Privacy

## What the Company Holds
The employee record in Workday holds identity and contact details, right-to-work documentation,
contract and compensation history, role and reporting history, performance records, leave and
absence history, and emergency contacts. Medical information is held separately by Occupational
Health and is not visible to managers or to HR generalists.

## Who Can See What
Employees can view their own complete record. A reporting manager sees role, leave balance, absence
dates, and performance records for their direct reports, but not bank details, medical information,
or salary history from previous roles. HR Operations sees the full record. Access is logged, and
logs are reviewed quarterly.

## Keeping Records Accurate
Employees are responsible for keeping personal details current in Workday, including address,
emergency contact, and bank details. Changes to legal name, marital status, or dependants require
supporting documentation and are processed by HR Operations within five business days. A correction
to a factual error can be requested at any time through the HR Service Desk.

## Retention
Active employee records are retained for the duration of employment. After the last working day,
core records are retained for seven years to meet tax, pension, and employment-law obligations.
Unsuccessful applicant data is retained for 12 months. Recorded interview material is deleted after
six months. Records past their retention period are deleted on a quarterly schedule.

## Access Requests
An employee may request a copy of the personal data held about them by raising a subject access
request through the HR Service Desk. The request is fulfilled within 30 calendar days at no cost.
Where a record contains information about another person, that portion is redacted rather than the
request being refused.

## Confidentiality and Breach Reporting
Employee data may only be accessed for a legitimate business purpose. Looking up a colleague's
record out of curiosity is a disciplinary matter. Suspected loss, exposure, or unauthorised access
to employee data must be reported to the Data Protection Officer within 24 hours of discovery, even
where the exposure is believed to be minor or already contained.
